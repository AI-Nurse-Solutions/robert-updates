## 1. The idea to preserve

Turn the monitor from a place to read AI answers into a large, interactive thinking workspace. A nurse speaks an idea and sees editable diagrams, workflows, relationships, and visual objects appear. The nurse can then use simple hand gestures, detected by a sensor or camera, to move objects, draw connections, group items into boxes, and expand or collapse details.

The long-term visual concept includes a large translucent or transparent display, creating the impression of manipulating ideas in space. Preserve that ambition, but do not make special display hardware a prerequisite for testing the interaction.

**Working principle: Speak the intention. See the structure. Shape the relationships. Keep decisions human.**

This is a proposed visual mode of Mission Control, not a replacement for Nurse AI OS and not a new autonomous decision-maker. No prototype or integration is claimed by this record.

## 2. Core experience

**Voice expresses meaning.** Create objects and groups, describe relationships, ask for alternatives, explain a dependency, or request a missing-step review.

**Gestures manipulate space.** Point, select, move, connect, group, expand, collapse, and zoom. Start with a small gesture vocabulary and short, comfortable movements rather than continuous raised-arm interaction.

**Conventional controls remain available.** Mouse, keyboard, touch, or stylus should provide alternatives. Voice-first does not mean voice-only; hand tracking must not become an accessibility requirement.

**Objects remain editable.** A box is not merely a drawing. It can hold a label, notes, source references, status, an optional owner, and relationships. Rearranging the canvas must not silently change the meaning or authorization of the underlying plan.

**Ambiguity is visible.** For an instruction such as “connect this to that,” highlight the selected objects and preview the proposed relationship. Allow correction and undo instead of silently guessing.

## 3. First demonstration scenario

Use a hypothetical, nonclinical community digital-skills workshop connected to the Compassion Engine discussion.

1. Say: “Show the community need, potential partners, resources, volunteers, and activities.”
2. Generate five editable groups.
3. Expand Resources into Needed, Requested, Offered, and Confirmed.
4. Move a potential partner beside an activity and propose an explicitly unconfirmed connection.
5. Ask: “What must happen before we announce the workshop?”
6. Review the dependencies, correct an interpretation, and save and reopen the editable plan.

People and commitments remain hypothetical or explicitly approved. The demonstration sends no invitations and makes no external commitments.

## 4. Relationship to Nurse AI OS

**Knowledge:** evidence and source cards support the objects on the canvas.

**Judgment:** assumptions, uncertainties, alternatives, and missing perspectives become visible.

**Capability:** an approved plan may later be translated into bounded tasks through a separately authorized workflow.

**Contribution:** community missions can connect approved collaborator profiles, offered resources, and shared outcomes.

The Compassion Engine can prompt questions about dignity, inclusion, community participation, and sustainable volunteer workload. The people directory and agent connections are possible later integrations, not prerequisites for the first prototype.

## 5. Proposed development sequence

**First: voice plus an ordinary display.** Test creating, selecting, editing, grouping, connecting, undoing, saving, and reopening a structured canvas. Use conventional controls before adding hand tracking.

**Next: simple hand tracking.** Add deliberate selection and a few spatial gestures. Test calibration, incorrect selections, unintended gestures, interruption, and comfortable seated use.

**Later: large or transparent displays.** Evaluate readability, background distraction, viewing distance, mounting, ergonomics, and cost only after the interaction demonstrates useful value. No hardware purchase is authorized.

The libraries and devices mentioned during the conversation are research candidates only. No vendor, model, protocol version, hosting arrangement, license, or security capability is selected or verified by this brief.

## 6. Boundaries and evaluation

**Drawing a connection is not authorizing an action.** Moving a person's card does not enroll them. Connecting an agent does not grant permissions. Sending, sharing, spending, or executing requires a separate explicit approval.

Use synthetic or public, non-sensitive material in the initial prototype. Keep patient information, confidential employer data, private memories, and unapproved personal information out. Define microphone and camera consent, visible capture controls, processing location, and retention before enabling capture; local processing is a design option to evaluate, not an implemented guarantee.

Evaluate against an ordinary whiteboard or chat-based workflow: clarity of the resulting plan, missing dependencies noticed, time and corrections required, recovery from misunderstandings, physical effort, and willingness to reuse. Save/reopen fidelity and the absence of accidental external actions are also acceptance questions. Set numerical targets before any pilot rather than inventing results now.

Pause or simplify if gesture errors, physical effort, privacy concerns, or added complexity outweigh the thinking benefit. Do not scale based on appearance alone.
